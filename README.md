A two-line entry, written by `main.ts` to `/output/entry.txt` — nothing else.

## The entry

> When Grandfather asks you to stay for tea on his seventy-ninth birthday, stay — the report will still be there on Monday, and he will not.
> That was the last time he told the river story in his own voice; every version since has been mine, and mine is thinner.

## Why these two lines

- **A moment, not a slogan:** the field saturates with "don't send that 2am text", "don't delete dad's voicemail" and "don't deploy on Friday". This one names a person, a numbered birthday and a pot of tea — a scene the reader can stand inside.
- **The reason is inside line 1:** the report will still be there on Monday, and he will not. The trade is stated in the same breath as the instruction, so the past self hears the whole argument at once.
- **Line 2 pays it off concretely:** that afternoon was the last telling of the river story in his own voice; every retelling since is second-hand and thinner. What was lost is specific — a voice, not a feeling.
- **Inside the gate:** 259 characters against the 280-character gate, exactly two lines.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts            # writes /output/entry.txt
OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```
